He uses only two modes, medium for executing a spec another model planned and xhigh for planning and investigation, and medium is also his orchestrator default because ambiguous problems belong to workers, not the first mate. His earlier "model size is wisdom, reasoning intensity is diligence" experiment (running big models at low effort) is superseded by this post; do not route a well-defined task at low effort on its authority.
- Quota stewardship (2026-09-27): use quota fully but steadily, so work never stops, the captain never waits on a reset, and no token is wasted. Principles, not fixed numbers - derive every figure from live `quota-axi --full` data at decision time:
  - Pace to the tightest window. Every provider has several windows (e.g. 5-hour and weekly); plan against whichever runs out first, aiming for a burn multiple near 1x so the allowance lasts until its reset. A short window with room can still hide a long window that is running out.
  - Measure before and after every change in concurrency: look at the burn rate a change actually produces instead of assuming it, and correct early.
  - Size concurrency to pace: add a worker only when every relevant window has pace headroom; when pace runs hot, stop adding and let natural task boundaries shrink the fleet.
  - Keep a 20% safety reserve on the Claude account signed in since 2026-09-29 (thakur.abhi3507; captain: "reserve 20% quota in this"), and 10% on every other provider (captain, 2026-09-27), watched automatically (`bin/fm-procevent-quota.sh arm --provider <p> --threshold 10`), re-armed each session and after each alert; use quota down to that floor, which is a backstop, not the pacing tool.
  - Spread work across providers by headroom, but only at task boundaries; route through Jev and `config/crew-dispatch.json` at task level and never switch a model mid-session.
  - Protect invested context: when something must pause, pause the worker with the least sunk context, never one that is deep into a task; never relaunch without a concrete reason.
  - Scale effort to task definition (medium when well defined, xhigh when not) and keep briefs token-efficient; quality is never traded away - every deliverable meets `docs/UNIVERSAL-STANDARDS.md`.
  - The orchestrator spends the same quota: the primary firstmate runs on Claude Sonnet 5.5 (captain setting since 2026-09-29), still on the Claude account, so Claude drains faster than Codex; worker placement must count that load, preferring Codex for planning work whenever live quota shows Claude burning faster, and firstmate itself keeps turns and replies short, and never polls or re-reads needlessly.
